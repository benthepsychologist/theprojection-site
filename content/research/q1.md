# Q1 — Where is the money going?

The hyperscalers and frontier labs are spending on the order of
three-quarters of a trillion dollars a year on the AI buildout. What does
that actually buy — chips, land, power, cooling, networking, labor — and
how much of it is the industry paying itself?

This page is a snapshot of an ongoing flow map, not a finished answer. It
tracks named entities (companies, funds, joint ventures) as nodes, each
split by *activity* — a company's capital-raising facet is a different
node from its data-center-construction facet, because money and physical
buildout are different questions — connected by dated, sourced edges:
who paid whom, how much, for what, and how sure we are of it.

**As of 2026-08-12: 189 nodes, 72 edges**, each edge carrying at least one
full observation (amount, sourcing, a confidence score) rather than a bare
number. This is real, cited data — every figure below links to a primary
filing, a company's own release, or reported deal coverage, graded
accordingly — not a modeled estimate.

The single biggest fact in that second table: over a quarter-trillion
tracked dollars sits at "financing itself" — raised, not yet spent on a
named physical thing. That's not a gap in this map's coverage; it's a
real property of how this buildout runs. Capital gets raised in large,
publicly-disclosed tranches (stock offerings, credit facilities, bond
sales) well ahead of the specific fab, chip order, or data-center lease
it eventually funds, and the two events are usually reported separately,
if the second one is reported at all.

## What moved this week

Five new financings entered the map on 2026-08-11–12, none previously
tracked:

- **Nvidia partnered with six of the largest asset managers** — Apollo,
  BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR — to establish
  financing platforms aimed at mobilizing **over $500B of third-party
  capital** for AI compute infrastructure, with Nvidia itself committing
  to potentially backstop up to **$125B** of that total. This is a
  framework, not a completed transaction: no dollars have moved to a
  named borrower yet, so the $500B figure is recorded as a target, and
  only Nvidia's own $125B commitment is carried as a real dollar edge.
  ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-partners-with-apollo-blackrock-blackstone-brookfield-goldman-sachs-and-kkr-to-establish-ai-compute-infrastructure-financing-platforms-to-mobilize-over-500-billion-of-third-party-capital))
- **CoreWeave disclosed a second, separate $3.1B delayed-draw term loan**
  at its Q2 earnings — on top of the $2.6B facility already tracked from
  four days earlier — carrying a 1.35x minimum debt-service-coverage
  covenant from September 2026. The same quarter's net interest expense
  more than doubled year-over-year, to $640M.
  ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000362/crwv-20260811.htm))
- **Intel raised $20B in common stock**, upsized from an initial $15B
  announcement on reported 5x institutional demand — its first entry in
  this map's capital-flow tracking; previously only its CHIPS Act

  ([Intel](https://newsroom.intel.com/corporate/intel-announces-upsize-and-pricing-of-20-billion-common-stock-offering))
- **Lambda priced a $917M leveraged loan**, led by Morgan Stanley and
  reported nearly $2B oversubscribed, to buy Nvidia GPUs — a second
  neocloud, after CoreWeave, financing chip purchases from Nvidia with
  debt while Nvidia is simultaneously an investor in, supplier to, and

  ([TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/lambda-917-million-gpu-leveraged-loan-nvidia-morgan-stanley))
- **TSMC's board approved roughly $29.4B in new capital appropriations**
  for advanced-node capacity and packaging, alongside a Sony image-sensor

  ([TSMC board announcement](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/tsmc-board-approves-us29-4-billion-capex-q2-dividend-and-sony-image-sensor-jv))

## Coverage, honestly

Of this map's 189 nodes: **144 measured** (a real, disclosed figure
attached), **10 aggregated** (a real figure that mixes AI and non-AI
activity with no way yet to split it, like TSMC's construction node),
**6 unmeasured** (a named entity with no independently-researched figure
yet — mostly co-arrangers added only because they're named on someone
else's financing, not yet researched in their own right). The rest are
round/financing-event nodes, which don't carry a coverage state of their
own.

Every dollar figure on this page is graded by how it was sourced — a
primary SEC filing or company release fetched directly scores highest; a
figure relayed through a news aggregator, or through this map's own
research process rather than an independent re-check, scores lower and
is marked as such in the underlying record. Two figures added this week
(TSMC's capex, Google's bond target) sit at the lower end of that scale
and are flagged for a follow-up pass against a primary source before
they should be treated as fully settled.
