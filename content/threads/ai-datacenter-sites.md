## 2026-09-24 — Oracle tells Project Jupiter's developer it may invoke force majeure

- **Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the developer of its Project Jupiter data-center campus in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, a unit of Blue Owl Capital, to protect itself from higher costs and defer payments if the campus is not operating by 2028, Bloomberg reported Thursday.** Oracle told CNBC "Project Jupiter remains on our planned schedule" and that it is "fully committed to New Mexico"; Blue Owl said the notice "does not change the financial commitments to this multi-year project." Oracle shares fell about 4% in early trading. The campus, part of the Stargate buildout with OpenAI, has faced permit litigation, a power plan redesigned around fuel cells, and about $18bn of project loans trading at stressed levels. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/oracle-data-center-force-majeure.html), [Roic](https://www.roic.ai/news/oracle-sends-force-majeure-notice-over-new-mexico-data-center-09-24-2026))

## 2026-09-23 — Chicago's mayor proposes a one-year data-center moratorium

- **Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson introduced an ordinance on 09-23 for a 12-month moratorium on new data centers in the city.** It is cosponsored by Alds. Maria Hadden, Lamont Robinson and Leni Manaa-Hoppenworth and follows an August executive order that created a city task force and told departments to intensify scrutiny of new projects; Hadden said the pause "doesn't even have to last the year" if rules are ready sooner, and Johnson is heading into a re-election campaign. ([Chicago Tribune](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2026/09/23/mayor-brandon-johnson-chicago-data-center-moratorium/))
- **Chicago Ald. Bill Conway (34th) introduced his own data-center ordinance the same day Mayor Brandon Johnson's 12-month moratorium reached City Council, offering zoning restrictions and a Plan Commission review process instead of a citywide pause, while Ald. Marty Quinn sent Johnson's moratorium to the Rules Committee — a maneuver Block Club Chicago said is often used to delay or kill legislation.** Conway's version would ban new data centers in some commercial corridors and parts of Downtown and require a Planned Development zoning process (triggering a community meeting and Plan Commission approval) elsewhere; he cited a data center moving forward at 400 S. LaSalle St. in his own ward with no community input, saying "a nail salon has more oversight than a data center does right now." The Data Center Coalition industry group called Johnson's moratorium a signal Chicago is "closed for business," warning it would drive away union construction jobs. ([Block Club Chicago](https://blockclubchicago.org/2026/09/23/year-long-ban-on-new-data-centers-pitched-by-chicago-mayor-brandon-johnson/))

## 2026-09-22 — Data Center Watch's Q2 count: $68bn/45 projects blocked or delayed, with 843 opposition groups now active in 49 states

- **Research group Data Center Watch counted roughly 45 US data-center projects worth $68bn blocked or delayed by local opposition in Q2 2026 (April–June), and identified 843 organized opposition groups now active across 49 states (all but Hawaii)** — the direct follow-on to this thread's own 08-31/09-04 entry logging the same group's Q1 2026 figure ($130bn across 75 projects). The Q2 total is a smaller dollar figure than Q1 but confirms the pattern this thread has tracked case-by-case all year (Cary, Bradley County, Mona UT, Prince William, San Francisco, and others) is now a measured national trend, not a string of isolated fights: roughly 30 state legislatures have introduced or adopted rules on data-center siting, power or water this year. Widely corroborated same-day/next-day (Bloomberg, Tom's Hardware, Breitbart, IBTimes, Yahoo). ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/new-data-centers-worth-68-billion-disrupted-in-us-data-show), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/local-opposition-blocked-usd68-billion-worth-of-data-center-projects-in-the-second-quarter-of-2026-data-center-investments-reportedly-still-on-track-to-hit-usd32-trillion-by-2050))

Supervisors kept a 90-day transition period for some properties already inside the overlay (the commission had urged 120), and a motion to drop the grace period entirely failed. ## 2026-09-08 (late catch, added 2026-09-14) — A four-minute hearing five months ago approved a $10bn Utah campus; residents only just found out

- **Juab County's Planning Commission approved a conditional-use permit for "Project Nebo" — developer SetSquare Capital Group's roughly $10bn, ~3-million-sq-ft data center campus near Mona, Utah, needing up to 750MW from Rocky Mountain Power — on 04-15-26, in a hearing residents say lasted four minutes and carried no mailed notice** (conditional-use permits, unlike zoning changes, require none under county rules). Opposition organized only once construction plans became public months later: over 300 residents joined a "Mona Cares: Neighbors for Responsible Development" Facebook group, at least 100 protested outside a 09-08 Juab County Commission meeting at Rocky Ridge Town Hall, and a Deseret News poll found 93% of surveyed county residents opposed.
