## 2026-09-11 — The bond selloff turns explicitly global, and Lagarde names AI financing as a driver of the long-end stress

- **The US 10-year's move to 4.954% Friday came inside a selloff that is global rather than American — German Bunds near 3.34–3.36%, French 10-year OATs at 4.34%, and UK 30-year gilts at 5.89%, their highest since May 1998 — one day after the ECB became the first of the Fed/BOJ/ECB trio to actually hike rather than hold.** This is the cross-border leg this thread exists to track finally showing up simultaneously across every curve it watches, not just Japan's. ⚠️ The US level is cross-checked; the European levels are aggregated from TradingEconomics and Bloomberg-sourced summaries rather than fetched line by line. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/us-treasurys-bonds-yields.html))
- ⚠️ **ECB President Christine Lagarde named AI financing needs directly as a driver of the long-end bond stress the Governing Council is "monitoring attentively" — "significant financing needs from AI is a key driver," in her words at Thursday's post-decision press conference.** This is the first time a central-bank official has stated the AI-capex-to-sovereign-yield channel out loud on this thread, rather than the map inferring it from correlation; markets are now pricing additional ECB hikes after Lagarde called Thursday's move a "no brainer." The quote is corroborated identically across two independent aggregations; the ECB's own transcript page 403'd on direct fetch, so this is aggregator-corroborated rather than read off the primary record. ([Investing.com/Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/lagarde-comments-at-ecb-press-conference-4895975), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-10/markets-bet-on-more-rate-hikes-as-lagarde-calls-latest-lift-a-no-brainer))

## 2026-09-10 — The ECB hikes to 2.5%, breaking this thread's "all three held" line six days before the Fed, and US PPI pushes hike odds to 63%

- **The European Central Bank raised its policy rate a quarter point to 2.5%, naming the Iran war as an inflation driver, and raised both its inflation forecast and its growth forecast in the same statement — President Christine Lagarde called the decision a "no brainer."** This thread's own standing snapshot (attention/capital-context.yaml, as of 2026-08-25) records that "Fed, BoJ and ECB all held rather than pivoting into a fresh tightening cycle despite oil-driven inflation pressure." The ECB is now the first of those three to hike, six days before the Fed's own 2026-09-16 decision. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/rates-bonds/ecb-hikes-rates-iran-war-adds-inflation-angst-2026-09-10/), [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/sep/10/ecb-raises-interest-rates-iran-war-inflation), [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/index.en.html))
- **US producer prices rose 0.4% month-on-month in August, in line with expectations, but the annual rate hit 5.4% — the highest reading of the year — driven by energy costs, with Reuters characterizing the headline as in line but "key details firmer."** Kalshi's odds on a 2026-09-16 Fed hike rose to 63% mid-afternoon, up from 60% at 10:00 ET and 49% on Wednesday, with Friday's CPI print now the stated swing factor. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/us-producer-prices-increase-expected-august-key-details-firmer-2026-09-10/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/ppi-inflation-report-august-2026.html))

## 2026-09-08 — The yen hits a seven-month high on BOJ hike bets, dragging the Nikkei down 1,130 points, and the dollar-index gap proves it is yen-specific

- **USD/JPY moved to 153.93 against Friday's 156.20 — a 1.45% stronger yen and a seven-month high — on bets the Bank of Japan hikes, and the Nikkei 225 fell 1,130 points (−1.70%) to 65,269.33 as the move unwound yen-funded carry positions.** The tell that this is a Tokyo-specific carry-unwind rather than broad dollar weakness: the dollar index fell only 0.31% over the same window, a fraction of the 1.45% yen move, so the currency pair moved on its own terms rather than the dollar moving against everything. This is the sharpest print yet on this thread's watch question — whether Japan's long end and the yen carry-trade unwind are now setting terms for risk assets independent of the US macro story — landing in the same session as an unrelated Gulf oil shock that a same-day read risked folding into one "risk-off" story. (Levels cross-checked against the settlement series, 2026-09-08.)

## 2026-09-04 — A yen carry-trade unwind pushes the currency to a one-month high into the BOJ's September decision

- **The yen strengthened into the 155-per-dollar range on 09-04, from around 157 the day before and a low near 158 earlier in the week, on speculation that the Bank of Japan will hike faster than expected at its 09-17/18 meeting and that yen-funded carry trades are being unwound.** Bloomberg led with "Carry Trade Exodus Fuels Yen Surge Ahead of BOJ Rate Decision," reporting traders rapidly closing yen-funded positions after Governor Ueda said hikes are on the table at every meeting and board member Takata's hawkish remarks, with Nomura sketching an "extreme scenario" of three consecutive hikes through December; Nikkei Asia reported the market reading a rare August meeting of Japan's public pension fund as a signal of more yen-asset buying. The 10-year JGB yield sat above 3%, the level it first crossed on 09-01. The move is the FX channel through which a Tokyo decision reaches US risk assets: the last comparable unwind, in August 2024, took the Nikkei down 12% in a day and the S&P 500 3% before the BOJ walked back its guidance. ([Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/markets/currencies/speculation-of-carry-trade-reversal-as-yen-strengthens-to-155-range), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/carry-trade-exodus-fuels-yen-surge-ahead-of-boj-rate-decision))
