## 2026-08-30 — France's under-15 ban was struck down as unconstitutional on 08-14, and this thread carried it as live law for twenty days

- **France's Conseil constitutionnel struck down Article 1 of the law protecting minors from social-media risks on 2026-08-14, voiding the under-15 ban itself before it could take effect.** The dispositif is unambiguous: *"L’article 1er de la loi visant à protéger les mineurs des risques auxquels les expose l’utilisation des réseaux sociaux est contraire à la Constitution."* The Council's reasoning goes directly to the question this thread has been tracking. It found the ban an *"interdiction of general scope"* depriving minors of access to numerous services without regard to the individual minor or the risks specific to each service, and — the operative point — held that requiring under-15s to be blocked necessarily implies **every** user, adults included, must prove their age, and that *"le législateur n’a pas prévu les garanties légales de nature à assurer le respect de ces exigences constitutionnelles"* — the legislature never defined the conditions or limits of that verification. **The ban did not fall because France chose the wrong verification method. It fell because the age-verification requirement it implied was left legally undefined.**
  ([Conseil constitutionnel, Décision n° 2026-911 DC du 14 août 2026](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/2026/2026911DC.htm), [communiqué de presse](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/actualites/communique/decision-n-2026-911-dc-du-14-aout-2026-communique-de-presse))

- ✏️ **Correction to this thread's 2026-08-10 entry, which is now wrong in its central claim.** That entry read "France requires hard ID verification instead of Australia's declaration model" and described a ban blocking new under-15 accounts from September 2026 with passport/licence/national-ID checks. **Four days later the Conseil constitutionnel voided the provision, and this thread went on carrying the claim for twenty days.** Two things are worth recording about how that happened rather than just fixing it. The 08-10 entry rests on a single source, Reclaim The Net — an advocacy outlet, not a primary text — for a claim about what a statute requires, where the statute and its constitutional review are both published and free to read. And the map's own expectations ledger flagged the uncertainty this morning but aimed it at the wrong document: it called for "a direct read of the enacted text on legifrance.gouv.fr," when the dispositive act was a Constitutional Council decision that no reading of the enacted text would have surfaced. **The comparison this thread was set up to run — hard-ID France against declaration-based Australia and Malaysia — cannot be run, because the French leg no longer exists.** Any narrower replacement statute would have to define its verification safeguards to survive review, which makes the eventual French model a stronger test than the one just lost, whenever it arrives.

## 2026-08-29 — The settlement's structure turns out to be a lever on TikTok and YouTube, not just a payment

## 2026-08-26 — Meta settles the Oakland trial for up to $17B before a verdict; the causality fight moves from courtroom to court-ordered product design

- **Meta agreed to pay up to $17 billion over ten years to settle the consolidated multistate trial in Oakland, and to make specific, injunctive changes to Facebook and Instagram, ending the trial before a jury verdict on the causality question this thread tracks.** California Attorney General Rob Bonta's office (the lead-state primary source) put the coalition at 51 attorneys general — all 50 states plus D.C. and U.S. territories — with California alone set to receive $1.5B–$2.1B if the settlement is approved. The announcement landed Wednesday 2026-08-26, roughly a week and a half into a trial that had been expected to run through early October.
  ([California DOJ — AG Bonta press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-secures-transformative-17-billion-settlement-meta), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/26/nx-s1-5944781/meta-settlement-child-safety-lawsuit))
- **The injunctive terms are the part this thread should weigh most — they're policy responses to the causality debate arriving by settlement rather than by resolved science:** a default 2-hour daily time limit for under-18 users (dropping to 1 hour only if Snap, TikTok and YouTube adopt matching caps — Meta's C.J. Mahoney explicitly called on them to follow), a midnight–6am nighttime block that only a parent can lift, notification blocks during nights and school hours, a ban on displaying "likes"/reaction counts on minors' posts, a ban on cosmetic-surgery filters for minors, a "non-personalized feed" option stripping algorithmic targeting, and an independent compliance auditor with attorney-general access. An injunction also bars Meta from making false or misleading safety claims going forward.
  ([California DOJ](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-secures-transformative-17-billion-settlement-meta))
- **⚠️ Not yet final, and not the end of the litigation.** U.S. District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers received the settlement document early Wednesday morning ("when it comes such a document at 6 a.m. ... it's good to make sure that I take a closer look") and declined to approve it immediately at the same hearing, though she called it "a good step forward" and said she expected to grant approval; the case remains suspended "until further notice" pending her review, not formally concluded. Law professor Nora Freeman Engstrom's read, quoted by NPR: "Meta is clearly signaling that it would like this litigation behind it. But this is not game over" — thousands of other individual harm suits against Meta are unaffected. Reported settlement totals vary by outlet ($17B–$18B) depending on whether a separately-reported ~$1B Texas settlement is folded in; that Texas figure is unconfirmed against a primary source as of this check.
  ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/26/nx-s1-5944781/meta-settlement-child-safety-lawsuit), [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/08/26/meta-reaches-18b-settlement-oakland-teen-safety-trial/))
- **Why this belongs on the causality thread specifically, not just as a Meta-liability story:** the thread's own framing is that policy keeps running natural experiments faster than academia resolves the underlying science. A $17B court-ordered product redesign, imposed on the causality debate's most-litigated platform without the jury ever reaching a verdict on whether Meta's design *caused* the harm, is the sharpest version yet of that pattern — regulation/litigation settling the practical question while the Haidt-vs-Odgers/Przybylski academic fight stays open. It also gives the thread a live natural experiment to watch: if the 2-hour cap and midnight block actually change adolescent wellbeing metrics over the next year, that is exactly the population-level data this thread has said the field lacks.

## 2026-08-25 — Psychologist Jean Twenge testifies in the Oakland trial that social media use likely caused a decline in adolescent wellbeing

## 2026-08-10 — France requires hard ID verification instead of Australia's declaration model

- **France's under-15 social-media ban (new accounts blocked starting
  September 2026) requires every user, not just minors, to verify age

## 2026 — A large within-person UK panel study also lands null on social media and mental health

## 2025-02 — School phone bans: real academic gains, null mental-health effects

- **A 91-school England study found a 6.4% test-score increase after
  phone bans**, concentrated among low-income students, plus improved
  attendance — but the same UK data found no association with wellbeing,
  anxiety, depression, or sleep. In-school phone use fell about 30
  minutes a day, but total use outside school compensated, so overall
  exposure barely moved even where in-school behavior clearly did — the
  academic and mental-health claims are being cited as one finding when
  the evidence says they're two separate ones.
  ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/02/250205131611.htm))

## 2025 — Sapien Labs: n≈28,000, earlier smartphone age correlates with worse adult wellbeing

- **Sapien Labs' Global Mind Project (n=27,969, ages 18-24, using 2023
  survey data) finds earlier first-smartphone age strongly correlates
  with worse adult mental wellbeing**, especially on a "Social Self"
  dimension, with suicidal-ideation, aggression, and derealization items
  all elevated in the early-smartphone group — consistent across world
  regions, and the effect is stronger in females. Large-N and
  cross-cultural, but still self-report and correlational, and run by an
  advocacy-adjacent tracking organization rather than an independent
  academic RCT — flag carried forward from the source crawl.
  ([Sapien Labs](https://sapienlabs.org/age-of-first-smartphone-tablet-and-mental-wellbeing-outcomes/))

## 2024 — ABCD Study: no evidence of drastic tech-linked change

- **The Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study — the largest
  US longitudinal youth brain-development study — found no evidence of
  drastic changes associated with digital-technology use**, a heavyweight
  null/small-effect data point on the skeptic side of the debate.
  ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-024-00902-2))

## 2023 — Vuorre & Przybylski: 72-country study finds no evidence of widespread harm

- **A 72-country study of Facebook adoption (n=946,798, 2008-2019 data)
  found no evidence of widespread psychological harm from social-media
  penetration, and small positive associations for younger users** —
  though the associations were small and didn't clear pre-registered
  credibility thresholds in every analysis. A companion piece extended
  the analysis into *Clinical Psychological Science* in 2024. This is the
  flagship evidence the 2026 rebuttal above directly challenges.
  ([Royal Society Open Science](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10410217/))
