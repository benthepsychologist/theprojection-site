## 2026-08-26 (evening) — Cook answers: "not about real estate paperwork," no White House reply yet

- **Lisa Cook broke her silence late Wednesday, denying wrongdoing through a letter from attorneys Abbe Lowell and Norman Eisen rather than in her own voice.** The letter argues "there is no legal basis" for removal a second time, calls the mortgage-paperwork issue "an inadvertent error," not fraud — noting "the President and a third of his cabinet ... reportedly did the same thing" — and frames the whole effort as "an attempt by President Trump to force the Federal Reserve to bend to his will" rather than a real cause dispute. As of this run, neither Trump nor the White House has responded on the record. ⚠️ Exact release time not independently pinned down: ABC's wire report says "late Wednesday," while Al Jazeera's writeup carries a 2026-08-27 dateline — both describe the same letter, not two events; recorded here under 08-26 on ABC's timing.

## 2026-08-26 — The Cook deadline passes without a public response

- **The White House's deadline for Lisa Cook to answer its removal notice
  arrived with no public response from her — no filed response, no new
  court action, no resignation, and no fresh statement from counsel,
  whose last on-record comment dates to 08-07.** The 08-05 letter gave
  her 21 days to submit evidence to the Director of Presidential
  Personnel; note this deadline runs to the White House, not to a court,
  and her underlying suit is separately back in federal district court
  after the Supreme Court's 5-4 June ruling blocked the first removal
  attempt on due-process grounds. Reuters reports the administration
  eyeing other board vacancies instead.
  ([Reuters, via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/cook-case-smolders-trump-other-100347841.html),
  [U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2026-08-26/as-cook-case-smolders-trump-has-other-paths-to-shape-fed-policy-team))

- **July PCE printed 3.7% year-over-year on the headline, a tenth above
  consensus, with core at 3.3% as forecast — and Treasury yields barely
  moved.** The 2-year fell about a basis point to 4.176%, the 10-year rose
  about two to 4.643%, the 30-year fell about one to 5.166%, with a crude
  selloff offsetting the hotter headline. A consensus-beating print that
  moves nothing suggests the hawkish hold it supports is already priced.
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/26/feds-preferred-inflation-gauge-shows-core-prices-rose-3point3percent-annually-in-july.html),
  [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/treasuries-edge-lower-before-pce-data-with-fed-risks-in-focus))

## 2026-08-24 — The funding mechanism behind the Treasury's long-end intervention surfaces: a ~$950bn cash cushion at the Fed

- **The funding mechanism behind the Treasury's long-end intervention was reported for the first time: a Treasury General Account balance of roughly $935-950bn.** CNBC's Steve Liesman, citing two senior Treasury officials, reported the TGA — the government's operating cash account at the Fed — is sitting roughly $350-400bn above the ~$550-600bn level targeted under the prior administration, and that this cushion is what stands behind the doubled buyback ceiling of up to $4bn per off-the-run issue announced 08-19. The map has carried Deutsche Bank's "soft-form financial repression" characterisation of that intervention for days; this is the first account of what actually funds it. ⚠️ A direct CNBC fetch was blocked, so this rests on two independent outlets carrying the same scoop rather than on primary text; the $935bn figure was cross-checked against a Daily Treasury Statement for 08-20 cited in secondary coverage.
  ([247wallst.com, citing CNBC](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/24/bessent-quietly-built-a-950-billion-war-chest-sitting-in-an-account-at-the-fed-now-we-know-what-its-for/),
  [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/24/bessent-1-trillion-treasury-general-account-bond-buybacks.html))
- **Treasury yields fell at the Monday open on that reporting, after the 30-year touched a fresh 19-year high overnight.** The 10-year fell to 4.694% (prior close 4.738%) and the 30-year to 5.228% (prior close 5.276%), down from an overnight print of ~5.33% on the 30-year — itself a new 19-year high. Multiple outlets framed the pullback as a reaction to the TGA/buyback reporting rather than a change in the fiscal picture. This lands three days before Jackson Hole (08-27 to 08-29) and four before Warsh's first keynote as chair (08-28), both of which the map is watching.
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/24/treasury-yields-warsh-jackson-hole.html))
- **AT 15:00 ET — the pushback arrived, and it is procedural rather than about the policy itself.** Analysts argue Treasury eroded its own "regular and predictable" tradition by disclosing the doubled buyback ceiling and the TGA funding mechanism **two weeks after the quarterly refunding statement and outside the normal deliberation channel**. Treasury's rebuttal is narrow and factual: no auction schedule changed, the first operation is not until **09 September**, and the full quarterly plan was already laid out on 08-19. ⚠️ **The word now attaching to the dynamic in commentary is "fiscal dominance"** — a materially stronger claim than "soft-form financial repression," because it asserts monetary policy is being subordinated to the fiscal financing need rather than merely leaned on. Recorded as a characterisation gaining currency, not as an established fact.
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/24/bessent-1-trillion-treasury-general-account-bond-buybacks.html),
  [Yahoo Finance / CNBC repost, 09:21 ET](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/bessent-quietly-built-950-billion-132130268.html))
- **AT 15:00 ET — yields went nowhere after the morning move, and no Fed voice spoke.** The 10-year held around 4.69–4.72% and the 30-year around 5.23%, essentially where the 10:00 ET reading left them; there were no FOMC-speaker headlines today. ⚠️ **One correction to how Thursday's Cook deadline is being carried:** the 08-26 date originates in a **White House letter dated 05 August** giving her 21 days to respond, not in anything that happened today. Today's coverage is preview, and so is everything on Warsh before his 08-28 keynote.
  ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/24/treasury-yields-warsh-jackson-hole.html))
