## 2026-09-02 — OpenAI's $80M Effingham community fund makes its first disbursement, $4.8M for a year of free school meals

- **OpenAI will cover breakfast and lunch for every Effingham County School District student for the 2026-27 school year, a $4.8 million grant that is the first spending decision from its $80 million Project Camellia Community Fund.** Superintendent Dr. Yancy Ford announced it at the district's board meeting; the free-meal program starts Tuesday, September 8. OpenAI's head of community engagement, Hank Evans, said meals were the thing the company "heard consistently" mattered most from residents during its community-input process. The remaining $75.2 million of the $80M fund is still undecided, pending further community input — this is a down payment, not the full package. United Way's Kim Dennis framed it as $1,000-$4,000 in annual savings for affected families.
  ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/openai-will-pay-48m-for-effingham-school-meals/), [WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/09/02/openai-cover-effingham-county-student-school-meal-costs-with-48m-grant/), [WJCL](https://www.wjcl.com))

- **Three Effingham County residents — Dr. Michelle Finch-Mincey, Lehman Mincey Jr., and Raymond Carver (a familiar name on this thread, quoted since 08-29) — filed suit in Effingham County Superior Court on September 2 seeking to void Ordinance 2026-352, the zoning change commissioners gave final approval on August 4 that opened industrial-zoned land to data centers, including the Project Camellia site.** The complaint, filed by Savannah attorneys E. Gregory Gilluly Jr. and Brent J. Savage, alleges the county skipped both public hearings state law and its own rules require — one before the Planning Board, one before the County Commission — before approving the change, and asks a judge to throw the ordinance out and bar the county from acting on it unless the required process is followed. The suit does not ask the court to stop Project Camellia itself or halt construction; plaintiffs frame it as a process challenge, not opposition to the data center. As of publication Effingham County had not filed a response. ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/effingham-county-residents-sue-over-data-center-zoning-change-openai-lawsuit/), [WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/09/03/effingham-county-residents-sue-county-over-data-center-zoning-ordinance-demand-transparency/))
- **The complaint alleges County Manager Tim Callanan signed a nondisclosure agreement with OpenAI OpCo LLC that prevented him from sharing information about the project with the public** — see the Corrections section below for how this sits against the existing record. It also alleges commissioners weakened the Planning Board's role without following the county's own procedures, that an executive session was held without proper announcement, that resident information was shared with a private corporation, and possible conflicts of interest; none of these broader claims have been tested in court. Carver, quoted again here as he was at the 08-29 work session: "We're constantly being pushed out of being able to have a voice in this entire situation." ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/effingham-county-residents-sue-over-data-center-zoning-change-openai-lawsuit/), [WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/09/03/effingham-county-residents-sue-county-over-data-center-zoning-ordinance-demand-transparency/))

## 2026-09-01 — Effingham's commissioners approve the $90M agreement, completing a 6-mill combined tax cut, over one dissent

- **The Effingham County Board of Commissioners approved the intergovernmental agreement distributing OpenAI's Project Camellia payments — $30M a year over three years, about $9M of it annually funding the equivalent of a 2-mill county tax cut — completing a combined 6-mill reduction across the county, school and IDA levies that officials call the largest property-tax cut in county history.** County officials estimate homeowners save over $1,000 a year once the state homestead credit is included. **Commissioner Beth Helmly cast the lone dissenting vote,** saying she supports the tax relief itself but was not willing to vote without more time to review the documents and get her questions answered — a narrower, process-focused objection than the moratorium sympathy she and Commissioner Burdette voiced at the 08-29 work session, and not a reversal of it. This is the county's own vote on the package whose IDA and school-board halves are already recorded here from 08-31.
  ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/local/effingham-county-approves-6-mill-property-tax-cut/))

## 2026-08-31 — Effingham's Industrial Development Authority approves a $90M OpenAI tax-relief package and cuts the school millage rate

- ⚠️ **Out of window** (this landed 08-31, the day before this run's window opens at 09-01 15:00 ET) **and not on the existing record** — checked the [Camellia](/threads/camellia/) thread timeline (last entry 08-29, the commissioners' work session) and every digest from 08-30 through 09-01 for "Effingham"/"Camellia"; none carry it. Flagging because it's a substantive, primary-sourced development the thread would otherwise skip a cycle on. **The Authority approved three measures on 08-31: an intergovernmental agreement sending $21M to the county and $9M to the school district within 30 days (up to $90M available over the agreement's three-year term) for infrastructure, water/sewer, job-creation and economic-development spending; a Project Camellia PILOT amendment fixing OpenAI's payment rate at 32 mills for 15 years regardless of future local rate changes; and a waiver of the Authority's own 2-mill economic-development tax for 2026.** The school board separately voted its FY2027 millage rate down 2 mills, from 18.45 to 16.45 — the county's case that residents see near-term tax relief from the deal, not just the site's own PILOT. Board member Trisha Boyett argued the fixed 32-mill structure yields more total tax revenue over the term than the fluctuating rate it replaces; resident Raymond Carver pushed back: "We're constantly being pushed out of having a voice in this situation" — the same secrecy/voice complaint this thread has tracked since 07-23.
  ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/effingham-industrial-authority-approves-openai-tax-relief-90m-deal-project-camellia-data-center-property-tax/))

## 2026-08-29 — The work session answers its own open question: no vote, an apology, and a moratorium now on the table

- **The Effingham County Board of Commissioners held its three-hour Project Camellia work session Saturday 08-29, 10:00-13:00 at the county administrative complex** — about 75 residents packed the chamber and another 25 listened from overflow. **No vote and no decision on the project itself came out of it.** Commissioners walked through the site's history, the concerns raised so far, and the county's case for the project's benefits, then took public input. Residents pressed on noise (a nearby homeowner: "we wanted the quiet right to enjoyment"), grid resilience during hurricanes (a resident citing existing 8-14 day outages and warning of "catastrophic failures"), utility-bill exposure, and — from an attorney — aquifer recharge areas and battery-storage siting on permeable soils.
- **Commissioner Roger Burdette, whose District 2 contains the site, broke from the county's messaging to admit the process was mishandled:** "I was wrong... If I would have a chance to do this again I would have done it differently." He and Commissioner Beth Helmly (District 4, who had already been pushing for a dedicated public forum) both said they'd consider an **18-month moratorium on new data centers** plus conditional-use-permit requirements for future projects — the same policy tool this thread has tracked landing elsewhere (Cary, NC; Wisconsin Rapids). **This is a first: county officials engaging the moratorium idea themselves, one day after Sen. Warnock's outside call for one.** No timeline was given for when that policy question would come back for an actual vote.
  ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/effingham-residents-press-commissioners-over-openai-data-center-protest/))

## 2026-08-28 (late catch, added 2026-08-29) — A US Senator visits the site and calls for a moratorium

- **Sen. Raphael Warnock (D-GA) toured the Belmont Glen neighborhood adjacent to the Camellia site and met privately with community stakeholders at Zion Lutheran Church in Guyton**, then told reporters the project should not proceed until the developer "can make guarantees on utility rates, on pollution, and transparency" — an explicit moratorium call. **This is the first federal-level political figure to weigh in on Camellia specifically**, escalating past the county-level opposition this thread has tracked since 07-30. He called the confidentiality agreements behind the project's pre-announcement land and tax-abatement moves (the subject of the Herald's "moved forward without public input" piece, same week) "the opposite of democracy," and said of stopping the project: "It's not impossible."
  ([WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/08/28/warnock-calls-data-center-moratorium-during-effingham-county-visit/), [Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/warnock-criticizes-lack-of-transparency-on-effingham-data-center/))

- 💡 **Sets up the 08-29 work session directly:** Warnock's visit and the Board of Commissioners' three-hour public work session (10:00-13:00, same administrative complex) landed one day apart, giving commissioners a live political pressure point walking into the session this map flagged as due today. **No outcome from the 08-29 session was available in same-day coverage as of this pass** — worth a direct follow-up check once local press files.

## 2026-08-27 — The final DRI report lands with real numbers, and two dates (late catch, event dated 08-26)

- **Georgia's Coastal Regional Commission released its final Development of Regional Impact report on Project Camellia**, described by the county manager as "the county's final requirement before it can approve Project Camellia's preliminary site plan." **This is the environmental disclosure the thread has been waiting for since the DRI was filed blank on 07-24:** roughly **65% of the site covered by buildings and pavement at full buildout**, with stormwater runoff, wetlands, floodplain and water-quality risks named and mitigation recommended. Two dates now set — a **county commissioners' public work session 08-29, 10:00-13:00** at 804 S. Laurel St., Springfield, and **preliminary site-plan review anticipated 09-14**. PSC Commissioner Peter Hubbard on scale: *"It's 32 times the size of the threshold... Something of this magnitude does deserve special scrutiny."*
  ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/openai-data-center-moves-forward-power-deal-approved-site-plan-review-next-dri-project-camellia/))

- **Georgia Power said its 3.2GW OpenAI contract was approved through the

- **Effingham commissioners denied signing any NDA with OpenAI** — at an
  Aug 4 meeting, Chairman Damon Rahn stated: "None of my commissioners
  signed any NDA's. We have no agreements with OpenAI," clarifying the
  county's role was limited to the Industrial Development Authority
  asking about water/sewer capacity before the project went public. The
  first time county leadership has responded directly to the local
  secrecy-backlash narrative this thread has tracked since 07-22.
  ([WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/08/05/effingham-county-commissioners-address-data-center-plans/))
- **County staff have received threats since the July 22 announcement** —
  Rahn confirmed it and said leadership issued a directive requiring
  staff to report all verbal/written/social-media threats to supervisors,
  HR and law enforcement — a new safety dimension not previously on file.
  ([WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/08/05/effingham-county-commissioners-address-data-center-plans/))
- **Commissioners also passed a zoning-language standardization ordinance**
  covering the industrial park where the data center will sit (Rahn's
  example: a mechanic shop had been miscategorized as "tire sales").
  ([WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/08/05/effingham-county-commissioners-address-data-center-plans/))
- **A dedicated public forum is now scheduled for late August** —
  Commissioner Beth Helmly pushed for a full Saturday devoted solely to
  Camellia, proposed for **Aug 22 or Aug 29** (venue/date still being
  finalized) — and for the first time, county leadership itself (not just
  petitioners) put a **moratorium on further data centers** on the
  agenda for that forum's discussion. A separate Chamber of Commerce
  panel is tentatively set for **Aug 26**, featuring OpenAI reps, the
  Industrial Development Authority, Georgia Tech and independent experts.
  No Coastal Regional Commission/DRI ruling yet — the ~30-day review
  clock (started 07-24) runs to roughly 08-23.
  ([Effingham Herald](https://www.effinghamherald.net/data-centers/project-camellia-effingham-plans-openai-data-center-forum/))

## ← Backstory

- **Site named** — Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub, a 2,600-acre industrial
  park near Rincon, Effingham County GA; the data-center build itself is
  ~101 acres across four buildings, already zoned industrial so no
  rezoning vote was required.
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/23/openai-data-center-project-sparks-outrage-among-effingham-county-residents/))
- **Power deal** — Georgia Power signed a 25-year PPA with OpenAI for
  3.2GW (phased 2028→2032, "over 2 million homes" worth), with OpenAI
  committing up to 1GW of flexible demand response back to the grid; the
  DRI filing left the actual generation mix blank as of 2026-07-25.
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/23/openai-data-center-project-sparks-outrage-among-effingham-county-residents/))
- **Developer of record** — filings name only Octans GA LLC, c/o Butler
  Snow (a Macon registered-agent/law firm); no general contractor or
  financial backer named, and no Oracle/SoftBank/MGX/Crusoe Stargate-JV
  structure appears anywhere in Camellia-specific coverage — reads as an
  OpenAI-direct build, structurally distinct from the Abilene TX Stargate
  consortium. (thin on partners — absence checked across ~10 outlets, no
  positive confirmation)
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/23/openai-data-center-project-sparks-outrage-among-effingham-county-residents/))
- **Public unveiling** — announced at a Rincon open house 2026-07-23
  (~1,000 attendees, ~300 protesting outside); a Change.org petition hit
  7,000+ signatures that evening and 10,000+ by the next day, with
  residents' core complaint being the secrecy of negotiations (no written
  deal existed "until the last minute," per a county commissioner) more
  than the project itself.
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/23/openai-data-center-project-sparks-outrage-among-effingham-county-residents/))
- **Deal terms** — 50% property-tax abatement for 15 years (after which
  OpenAI is expected to become the county's largest taxpayer), an $80M
  community benefits package (education/safety/health/small-business),
  up to $71M in Codex software credits for GA students, and 400 permanent
  jobs; approved by the Industrial Development Authority (a quasi-
  independent body) with no formal county commission vote.
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/23/openai-data-center-project-sparks-outrage-among-effingham-county-residents/))
- **Regulatory status** — county submitted a Development of Regional
  Impact (DRI) application 2026-07-24; next gate is a state Coastal
  Regional Commission review capped at 30 days, with engineering/fire/
  stormwater/noise review still pending after that. No formal
  groundbreaking date found — project is pre-construction as of
  2026-07-28.
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/25/as-effingham-county-data-center-plan-advances-next-step-is-state-evaluation-process/))
- **Cost figure unreconciled** — most local Georgia coverage cites $20B
  vs. Bloomberg's original "$30B+"; not resolved to a single number this
  crawl.
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/22/20-billion-openai-data-center-to-open-in-effingham-county/) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-22/openai-plans-to-spend-over-30-billion-on-georgia-data-center))
- **Not the SoftBank Ohio campus** — confirmed distinct: SoftBank's ~10GW
  Ohio project sits on the former DOE Portsmouth/PORTS uranium-enrichment
  site (Piketon, OH), with OpenAI reportedly leasing capacity there
  rather than building/owning as at Camellia; no outlet found conflates
  the two. (medium — RSS-metadata sourcing on the Ohio side, no
  publisher body text resolved)
  ([Data Center Dynamics via Google News RSS](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=SoftBank+Ohio+data+center+10GW))
- **Regional pattern** — Camden County passed a data-center moratorium in
  May 2026 and Bulloch County/Port Wentworth residents are separately
  pursuing one, suggesting Camellia-style local pushback is becoming a
  regional GA trend, not a one-off. (thin — one convergent mention)
  ([The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/23/openai-data-center-project-sparks-outrage-among-effingham-county-residents/))
